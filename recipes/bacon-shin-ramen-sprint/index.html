Bacon Shin Ramen

Deep bacon-and-chicken broth, browned mushrooms, tender cabbage, jammy eggs, and the real spicy flavor of Shin Ramyun. Built around your thick-cut bacon, chicken broth, mushrooms, cabbage, jalapeños, green onions, and ramen.

4 big bowlsdinner for everyone
30 minutesstart to finish
One potminimal cleanup

The exact ramen plan

Use 3 Shin Ramyun packs plus 1 chicken ramen noodle block to stretch this to four satisfying bowls. Use the Shin noodles and Shin seasoning; do not add the chicken ramen flavor packet.

Why only 2 Shin flavor packets? Bacon and chicken broth already contain salt. Two packets give the unmistakable Shin flavor without making the broth too salty. Add hot sauce or Tajín for more heat, not a third packet.

Cook it

1

Crisp the bacon

Put chopped bacon in a cold large pot or Dutch oven. Turn heat to medium and cook 8–10 minutes, stirring sometimes, until browned and crisp. Scoop out most of the bacon for topping. Leave about 1 tablespoon bacon fat in the pot; carefully pour off extra fat.

2

Brown the mushrooms, jalapeño, and spices

Add mushrooms and sliced jalapeño to the bacon fat. Cook 4–5 minutes without rushing so the mushrooms brown and the jalapeño softens. Add garlic, onion powder, paprika, black pepper, ginger, and optional red-pepper flakes. Stir just 30 seconds—until fragrant.

3

Build the Shin broth and soften the cabbage

Add all 8 cups chicken broth and 2 Shin seasoning packets. Bring to a full boil. Add the sliced cabbage and boil 2 minutes until it starts to turn tender. Taste the broth now: it should be savory, smoky, and spicy. Add 1 tsp soy sauce only if it needs more savory depth.

4

Cook the noodles

Add the 3 Shin noodle blocks and the 1 chicken ramen noodle block. Press them under the broth as they soften. Cook 3 minutes, gently separating the noodles with tongs or a fork.

5

Poach the eggs

Make four little spaces in the noodles and crack one egg into each. Cover the pot and cook 2–3 minutes for runny yolks; cook longer if you prefer firm yolks.

6

Finish bright

Turn off heat. Stir in 1 tsp vinegar; it will not taste vinegary—it wakes up the rich bacon broth. Divide into four bowls. Add bacon, green onions, and hot sauce or Tajín at the table.

Flavor fixes

Need more heat? Add hot sauce, Tajín, or red-pepper flakes to your own bowl. This increases heat without making the whole pot saltier.
Too salty? Add a splash of water or more unsalted broth. Do not add another Shin packet.
Leftovers: Store broth, bacon, mushrooms, and eggs separately if possible. Cook fresh noodles tomorrow; ramen noodles soften quickly in stored broth.
