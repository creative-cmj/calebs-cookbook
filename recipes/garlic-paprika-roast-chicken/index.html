Roast Chicken

Crisp paprika skin, juicy garlic-and-onion chicken, and optional cabbage roasted in the drippings. This guide is for a 4–6 lb thawed whole chicken—enough for four people with leftovers.

Important timing

Start hot, then finish gentler: roast at 425°F for 20 minutes, then reduce to 375°F until the thickest breast and inner thigh reach 165°F. A 4–5 lb chicken usually needs another 50–75 minutes; a 6 lb chicken can need 75–100 minutes. The thermometer decides—not the clock.

Roast it

1

Heat and dry

Heat oven to 425°F with the rack in the middle-low position. Remove neck/giblets if present. Pat the chicken extremely dry inside and out with paper towels; dry skin is what gets crisp.

2

Season all over

Mix salt, black pepper, paprika, garlic powder, onion powder, and Italian seasoning/rosemary. Rub the chicken with oil or melted butter, then rub on all of the seasoning. Gently loosen breast skin if you can and rub a little oil and seasoning under it too.

3

Fill the cavity—loosely

Put onion chunks, smashed garlic, and optional lemon inside. Do not pack it tight; loose aromatics perfume the chicken while air can still circulate.

4

Brown the skin

Set chicken breast-side up in the pan. Roast at 425°F for 20 minutes. This high heat starts the crisp, deeply flavored skin.

5

Finish at 375°F

Lower oven to 375°F. Roast until the thickest breast and inner thigh read 165°F, avoiding bone with the thermometer. Check around 50 minutes after lowering the heat. If using cabbage, add oiled/seasoned wedges around the chicken after 35–40 minutes of roasting.

6

Rest, then carve

Move chicken to a board, tent loosely with foil, and rest 15 minutes. This keeps the juices in the meat. Spoon pan juices over carved chicken and cabbage.

Quick saves

Skin browning too fast? Loosely tent only the browned top with foil; do not cover the whole bird tightly.
Need more color? If it is already 165°F but pale, broil 1–3 minutes while watching it constantly.
Leftovers: Refrigerate within 2 hours. Pull leftover meat from the bones for sandwiches, soup, or chicken ramen tomorrow.
